# Concepts

The vocabulary duly uses precisely, defined once each for readers new to this space. Entries are grouped by architectural layer, in the order a document travels through the system — division of labor, perception, the fact contract, time, adjudication, assurance, authoring, review — and each entry states what the term means *in duly* (sometimes narrower than the field's usage) and links to the source that carries the full argument.

## The division of labor

**Neuro-symbolic** — In the research literature this covers many hybrids, including systems where neural and logical components infer jointly. In duly it means one specific division of labor: a neural layer reads unstructured documents and *proposes* structured facts; a deterministic symbolic layer applies a versioned, effective-dated rulebase and *decides* what those facts mean. Perception proposes, logic disposes — there is no joint inference. Everything above the [fact contract](../spec/grounded-facts.md) is probabilistic and replaceable; everything below it is deterministic and replayable. Full rationale: [Why neuro-symbolic at all](../README.md#why-neuro-symbolic-at-all).

**Prior grounding (as opposed to post-hoc explanation)** — Evidence and constraints sit *in front of* the decision rather than being attached to it afterward. A retrieval-augmented system can show which passages were near the model when it answered — that is provenance of text, not provenance of the decision — and a generated rationale can read well without being the computation that produced the result. In duly a machine assertion must first become a typed, grounded [fact](#the-fact-contract), survive the admission checks, and be consumed by an explicit rule before it can influence anything; the [receipt](#adjudication) then records the actual rule-to-fact derivation. Sounding plausible, or citing a document, never confers decision authority — the assertion has to cross a governed interface first. Full treatment: [prior grounding rather than post-hoc explanation](neuro-symbolic-architecture.md#prior-grounding-rather-than-post-hoc-explanation).

## Perception

**Confidence and calibration** — A machine-asserted fact carries `confidence: {score, method}`, where the method is one of `raw`, `temperature`, `platt`, or `conformal`. In duly, calibration means fitting one of those methods on human-labeled `(raw_score, correct)` pairs: temperature scaling rescales logits with one parameter, Platt scaling fits a two-parameter logistic on the logit of the raw score, and conformal calibration does not remap the score at all — it certifies an abstention threshold (see the next entry). The labeled pairs come from the [review queue](../review/README.md) and are censored — they label only the facts that abstained — so on their own they do not calibrate the full score distribution; and nothing in the repository ships pre-calibrated. Details and caveats: [calibration/README.md](../calibration/README.md).

**Conformal guarantee (marginal vs. conditional)** — The guarantee a fitted conformal threshold supports is *marginal*: the probability that the system accepts a fact **and** the fact is wrong is at most α — finite-sample, distribution-free, assuming production traffic is exchangeable with the calibration pairs. It is deliberately not the *conditional* claim a dashboard naturally displays — the error rate *among accepted facts* — which is only bounded by α / P(accepted) and diverges from α when the system abstains a lot. duly's rule: report both, promise only the marginal one; drift (a new document template, a new extractor version) breaks the assumption and requires refitting. The precise statement and proof sketch: [calibration/duly_calibration/conformal.py](../calibration/duly_calibration/conformal.py).

**Extraction run envelope** — The content-addressed manifest of one extraction run over one [rendition](#the-fact-contract): the adapter's name and version, the source document's hash, the rendition's hash, and the ordered, complete list of fact ids the run proposed. It lets a consumer verify a whole run before ingesting anything — manifest hash, every fact's hash, every span against the rendition — and revoke a whole run at once by retracting every live fact carrying its `runId`. The hash is an integrity claim, not an authenticity claim; *who* produced the run is an open question. Spec: [resolved question 4](../spec/grounded-facts.md#resolved-questions); implementation: [extraction/duly_extraction/envelope.py](../extraction/duly_extraction/envelope.py).

## The fact contract

**Grounded fact** — The atom of the interchange format: one entity–attribute–value assertion — *(this notice, `nc:noticeMailedDate`, 2026-07-25)* — that must carry its grounding (a document span or an attestation), its calibrated confidence when machine-asserted, and its position on two time axes ([bitemporal](#time)). Values are typed, and numbers and money are decimal strings, never floats. Human corrections and machine extractions share this one shape; nothing but the `assertion` block differs. The contract, decision by decision: [spec/grounded-facts.md](../spec/grounded-facts.md).

**Grounding, quote span, and rendition** — Grounding is where a fact came from. A document grounding carries the document's SHA-256, the rendition it was read from, and a locator — a character span, a bounding box, or both — optionally with the quoted source text so receipts render without document access. Offsets point at a **rendition** — an immutable, content-addressed artifact recording the extractor's name, version, and the extracted text itself — rather than at "the PDF", because every extractor produces different text from the same bytes; old receipts keep resolving against old renditions after the extractor upgrades. Character spans count Unicode code points, end-exclusive. The [evidence browser](demo_tour.md#11-the-evidence-browser) shows the two artifacts side by side and highlights spans only on the rendition — a highlight drawn on the PDF would need coordinates no fact carries. See [D4](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d4-spans-reference-a-hashed-document-rendition) and [D3](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d3-every-fact-says-where-it-came-from-a-span-or-an-attestation).

**Attestation** — The non-span grounding, for facts that exist in no document because someone was told them: a human actor, a channel ("phone call with closing agent"), and a timestamp. It exists so such facts are not forced into fake document spans, which would corrupt provenance exactly where it matters. See [D3](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d3-every-fact-says-where-it-came-from-a-span-or-an-attestation).

**Content addressing (`contentHash`)** — A fact's identity derives from its bytes: `contentHash` is the SHA-256 of the fact's RFC 8785 (JCS) canonical JSON — excluding `id` and `contentHash` themselves — and the id is `urn:duly:fact:sha256:<hex>`. This is what lets receipts pin their inputs by hash so replay integrity is checkable byte-for-byte, makes identical facts deduplicate for free, and removes the need for any id-issuing authority. Receipts and run envelopes are addressed the same way. The canonical form is **one implementation** — [`duly_core`](../core/duly_core/) — with its answers frozen in [`spec/canonical-vectors.json`](../spec/canonical-vectors.json): eleven `(document, canonical bytes, digest)` triples any implementation in any language must reproduce, including the two everyone gets wrong (RFC 8785 orders object keys by UTF-16 code unit, not code point; non-ASCII is emitted raw rather than `\u`-escaped). See [D8](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d8-facts-are-content-addressed).

**PROV-O export** — The bridge to standard provenance tooling: external JSON-LD context files under which stored facts, receipts, and run envelopes — bytes unchanged, since they are content-addressed — expand to W3C [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) triples: a fact `prov:wasQuotedFrom` its rendition, a decision generated by an activity that `prov:used` each input fact under the rule pack as its `prov:Plan`. Any RDF/SPARQL engine can then answer lineage questions (*which decisions ever used a fact from this document?*) without a duly-specific importer. The mapping is deliberately partial: bitemporal effective time, confidence, and abstentions have no faithful PROV equivalent and stay in the `duly:` namespace. Spec and mapping tables: [spec/prov-o.md](../spec/prov-o.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/provo_sparql_demo.py](../spec/provo_sparql_demo.py).

**CURIE** — A compact URI: `prefix:name`, as in `nc:noticeMailedDate`, resolved against the ontology the fact declares in its `schemaRef`. CURIEs are how facts and rules name attributes and entity types without duly defining any of those terms itself — ontology by reference. See [D10](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d10-ontology-by-reference).

**Bring-your-own ontology** — duly ships no domain schema. Facts reference the user's ontology — MISMO, ACORD, FHIR, or project-local — by [CURIE](#the-fact-contract) plus version; duly validates conformance against that reference (the [conformance gate](#the-fact-contract), below) but defines no domain terms, a boundary that keeps the core from ever accumulating domain debt. The committed samples under [ontologies/](../ontologies/) are what a *user of the starters* brought, with a [walkthrough](../ontologies/README.md) for swapping in your organization's own. Rationale: [Why bring-your-own ontology](../README.md#why-bring-your-own-ontology).

**Ontology conformance gate** — The enforcement half of the previous entry: a fact's `schemaRef` pins an ontology name + version, and the gate resolves that pin against a registry of versioned, immutable LinkML artifacts, checking the entity type is a defined class, the attribute is declared on that class, the value's kind matches the declared range, and code values sit inside their declared code system (and, for closed enums, inside the permitted values). A misspelled attribute — which previously sailed through ingestion and surfaced only as a rule silently failing to bind — becomes a loud rejection naming the fact, the ontology version, and what failed, before anything reaches the store. Optional at the extraction seam (`verify_envelope(..., registry=...)`), additive everywhere else. Spec: [spec/ontology-conformance.md](../spec/ontology-conformance.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/conformance_gate_demo.py](../spec/conformance_gate_demo.py).

## Time

**Bitemporal (effective time vs. knowledge time)** — Two independent time axes. *Effective time* (`effectiveFrom`/`effectiveTo`) is when an assertion or a rule holds in the world; *knowledge time* (`recordedAt`) is when the system learned the assertion — assigned by the store, append-only, never edited. Both are needed to answer the defining query of regulated replay: "evaluate a March file under March rules, as we understood the facts in March." duly carries both from birth because retrofitting bitemporality onto a unitemporal store is a rewrite. See [D6](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d6-bitemporal-from-birth).

**Supersession** — No fact is ever edited. A correction is a *new* fact with `supersedes: <old fact id>`; the old fact's `superseded` (or `retracted`) status is a store-level projection, not a mutation of the original record. The supersession chain *is* the correction history — nothing about what was once believed is lost. The demo's [evidence browser](demo_tour.md#11-the-evidence-browser) is where that stops being a claim and becomes something you watch: its knowledge-time dial replays the store's event log, so dragging back one stop makes a correction not-yet-known and the fact it replaced live again. See [D7](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d7-facts-are-immutable-corrections-supersede).

**As-of replay** — Every adjudication runs against an explicit `asOf` pair — an effective date and a knowledge date — and the receipt records both, so re-asking the same question at the same pair reproduces the receipt byte-for-byte. The demo's outcome flip when the as-of date moves, and the assurance harness re-verifying 351 golden receipts on every push, are the same property exercised two ways. See [D6](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d6-bitemporal-from-birth) and [golden/README.md](../golden/README.md).

## Adjudication

**Rule pack** — A versioned, cited, effective-dated unit of domain knowledge: a `pack.yaml` holding the rules, the decisions the pack can answer *and how their answers are worded*, and an optional abstention policy, alongside an `expected.yaml` declaring adjudication outcomes — the pack's own test suite, discovered and run in CI. Every rule carries a legal citation (or an explicit `TODO(verify)`) and an effective window; anything that can change a decision, including abstention floors, is versioned with the pack so old receipts replay under old versions. Authoring guide: [rulepacks/README.md](../rulepacks/README.md); semantics: [spec/rule-ir.md](../spec/rule-ir.md).

**Defeasible rules (priority, overrides, defeat)** — Real rulebases are defaults and exceptions all the way down, so — following [Catala](https://catala-lang.org/)'s approach to computational law — rules carry exception structure declaratively. Two mechanisms, both recorded on the receipt: `overrides` lists the rules whose conclusions an exception suppresses when it fires, and `priority` breaks conflicts when two applicable rules conclude the same attribute (higher wins; equal priority must be provably disjoint or the pack validator rejects it). Each fired rule's receipt entry records the rules it **defeated**, which is what makes the non-monotonic reasoning itself auditable. See [Defeasibility semantics](../spec/rule-ir.md#defeasibility-semantics).

**Stratified evaluation** — The one-paragraph intuition: rules are evaluated in dependency layers, so a rule that consumes a derived value (a `derived` binding, like a required-minimum-days conclusion) runs after the rule that concludes it, and evaluation proceeds in dependency order to a fixpoint; dependency cycles are a pack-validation error, so evaluation always terminates deterministically. It is the same discipline as stratified Datalog, the IR's planned compilation target. Depth: [resolution semantics in spec/rule-ir.md](../spec/rule-ir.md#given--variable-bindings) and [Why a defeasible IR compiled to stratified Datalog](../README.md#why-a-defeasible-ir-compiled-to-stratified-datalog).

**Abstention** — The kernel declining to use an attribute, recorded on the receipt with a reason: `low_confidence` (a machine-asserted fact scored below the pack's confidence floor) or `conflict` (two live facts assert the same attribute and rank cannot resolve them). Abstention is *policy, not data*: floors live in the pack's versioned `abstentionPolicy` and a fact never carries an "abstained" flag, because the same fact may clear one pack's floor and not another's. Human-asserted facts are never confidence-filtered, and entries carry the `routedTo` queue responsible for them — abstention is the designed alternative to confident wrongness, an "I don't know" that goes somewhere. See [Abstention policy](../spec/rule-ir.md#abstention-policy) and [D5](../spec/grounded-facts.md#d5-confidence-is-a-calibrated-score-plus-its-method-abstention-is-policy-not-data).

**Decision phrasing** — How a pack words the *answer*, alongside the `question` it already declares: an ordered list of guarded cases giving a verdict, a supporting clause, and a tone, with placeholders for the decided value, a consumed fact, a derived intermediate, or the confidence caveat. It exists so authoring a pack is the whole job — before it, a new non-boolean decision rendered as a raw `attribute = value` until someone edited the demo's renderer, which put legal wording in the one directory lawyers never read. Phrasing is versioned with the rules and deliberately *outside* the receipt hash: a decision's meaning is `decision.value`, which is hashed; how it reads must stay free to improve. See [Decision phrasing](../spec/rule-ir.md#decision-phrasing).

**Permissibility window** — What an orchestrator gets when it asks the same question across a range of candidate dates: the set of days on which a decision comes back permitted, *read off the adjudications* rather than derived from the rule. A window is not a duly artifact — no schema, no hash, no standing — it is a shape a consumer builds out of receipts, and its authority is entirely the authority of those receipts. The distinction matters because the alternative is computing the window from the rule directly, which is the moment a second copy of the rule comes into existence. Worked example, including the case where the window is a *relation* rather than an interval (funding permissibility depends on the signing date, because the deadline is computed from it): [examples/closing-scheduler](../examples/closing-scheduler/README.md).

**Decision receipt** — The kernel's output, and the product: the conclusion plus everything needed to defend and replay it. A receipt records the `asOf` pair, the exact rule-pack version, every rule fired with its citation, priority, and `defeated` list, the derivation tree (each conclusion, the rule that produced it, and its premises), every input fact pinned by id and `contentHash`, every [abstention](#adjudication) with its reason and routing, and the engine that ran — all content-addressed like facts. Rendering the derivation tree as prose is the audit report. See [Decision receipts](../spec/grounded-facts.md#decision-receipts).

**Decision-semantics version (`engine.version`)** — The version inside a receipt's `engine` block, and therefore inside its hash. It names the kernel's *decision semantics* — what running the rules means — and is pinned as a constant, deliberately decoupled from the `duly_kernel` package version and the `duly` distribution version, which move with releases while sealed receipts cannot. The three scopes nest one way only: a semantics change implies a kernel code change implies a release, never the reverse. Like a [rule id](../spec/rule-ir.md), the sealed value is a handle, not a claim. It is also the **scope of the replay promise**: a receipt sealed under semantics V replays byte-identically under any kernel implementing V, a kernel may implement several, and one meeting a version it does not implement *refuses* rather than replaying and possibly agreeing by coincidence. That is why growing a new IR construct costs a semantics version even though it breaks nothing: a receipt using it would claim a version whose honest implementations cannot reproduce it. Which number moves for a given change: [docs/release-process.md](release-process.md); the promise itself: [spec/compatibility.md](../spec/compatibility.md).

**Decision digest** — A hash over the receipt fields that *determine the adjudication*, as distinct from `receiptSha256`, which identifies the **artifact**. Two runs that reached the same conclusion by the same rules from the same facts, on different machines or different evaluation backends, produce different receipt hashes and the same decision digest — because `caseId`, `rulePack.gitCommit`/`url`, `engine.kernel` and `engine.backend` are excluded, and everything excluded identifies the run rather than the decision. `engine.version` is *included*, since different semantics can genuinely mean different decisions. This is what makes cross-backend agreement definable at all: a second backend cannot produce byte-identical receipts by construction, so **two receipts agree when their digests match**, and byte equality is agreement plus having been the same run. It is deliberately a function and never a field — a digest stored inside the body it digests would be the extension point the receipt schema refuses. Definition and exclusion arguments: [spec/compatibility.md](../spec/compatibility.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/compatibility_demo.py](../spec/compatibility_demo.py); vectors: [spec/decision-digest-vectors.json](../spec/decision-digest-vectors.json).

**Calendar (pack-embedded)** — A named business-day definition — excluded weekdays, explicit holiday dates with source citations, and a hard coverage window — carried inside a rule pack's `calendars:` block, so it is versioned, receipt-pinned, and replayable exactly like the rules that use it. `add_business_days` in the rule IR walks these calendars; "last Monday in May" is legal content, not engine code. See the Calendars section of [spec/rule-ir.md](../spec/rule-ir.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/calendar_demo.py](../spec/calendar_demo.py).

**DMN decision table** — An OMG-standard tabular rule format: input columns, one output column, one row per rule, and a *hit policy* saying how overlapping rows resolve. duly treats it as an authoring surface, not an engine: [`duly_dmn`](../dmn/duly_dmn/) compiles a DMN 1.3+ table into a rule-IR pack, which the kernel then executes like any other. The compiler is deliberately narrow — [S-FEEL](#adjudication) cells only, three of DMN's seven hit policies, and a required citation and effective date on every row — because a table that compiles into a rule duly cannot justify is worse than a table that does not compile. Spec: [spec/dmn.md](../spec/dmn.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/dmn_demo.py](../spec/dmn_demo.py). A `UNIQUE` table the compiler refuses can be diagnosed with [static pack verification](#assurance), which says whether the rows genuinely overlap or merely cannot be proven disjoint by the kernel's narrow proof set.

**S-FEEL** — "Simplified FEEL", the DMN expression subset decision-table cells are defined over: literals, comparisons, ranges (`[1..5]`, `(0..10]`), comma-separated alternatives, `not(...)`, and the "irrelevant" marker `-`. duly's compiler accepts S-FEEL and refuses the rest of full FEEL — iteration, quantification, conditionals, contexts, function invocation other than `date(...)` — naming the decision, row, column, and cell text. The refusal is the feature: a silently dropped condition is a rule that fires more often than its author believes.

## Assurance

**What-if query** — Running the rulebase backwards. Free one input of a decided case — a fact's value, or the evaluation point itself — and ask which values reach a given outcome, plus the extremal one: *the notice had to be mailed by 2026-04-24*; *funds may not move until 2026-02-18*. The answer is a claim about **one execution that never happened**, which is why it is never trusted as it comes out of the solver: `python -m duly_whatif` re-adjudicates every proposed value through the real kernel, and boundary-verifies an extremal by checking the value passes *and* one step beyond it fails. A disagreement between solver and kernel raises with both artifacts rather than returning an answer. Two honest asymmetries: UNSAT ("no value works") is not pointwise-verifiable, because there is no point to check, and extremality itself rests on the encoding rather than on the kernel. Spec: [spec/whatif.md](../spec/whatif.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/whatif_demo.py](../spec/whatif_demo.py).

**Boundary verification** — The check that makes an extremal answer worth more than the solver's word: run the kernel on the claimed extreme *and* on one step past it, and require the first to reach the target and the second to miss it. Verifying only the answer is not enough — a broken encoding can return a value the kernel happily confirms while the true boundary sits a day away, which is precisely the failure a committed test in [`whatif/tests`](../whatif/tests) injects to prove the guard trips. See [spec/whatif.md](../spec/whatif.md).

**Static pack verification** — Asking a solver what the pack validator cannot answer. The kernel accepts exactly two disjointness proofs for same-priority rules — disjoint effective windows, or contradictory quoted-string equality guards on the same *attribute* binding — so a boolean split, a numeric range, or a guard on a derived value is unprovable to it, and authors fall back to an `overrides` or a manufactured priority gap. `python -m duly_assurance prove` encodes a pack as SMT and returns, per pair, PROVED-DISJOINT, NOT-PROVED with a concrete [witness](#assurance), or OUT-OF-FRAGMENT naming the construct it declined to encode. It also reports *coverage*: the input regions where no rule concludes a declared decision attribute — which is how the recording pack's intentional fail-closed hole for unencoded jurisdictions becomes a checked fact rather than a comment. Validation-time only: `z3-solver` is an optional extra, no solver output enters a receipt, and the kernel's own check is unchanged. Spec: [spec/pack-verification.md](../spec/pack-verification.md); runnable demonstration: [spec/prove_demo.py](../spec/prove_demo.py).

**Witness (verification)** — A concrete assignment of inputs demonstrating what a proof failed to rule out: the fee amount at which two rules both fire, the jurisdiction for which no rule concludes. A witness is what makes NOT-PROVED actionable rather than merely discouraging — *unproven* and *wrong* are different claims, and only a witness establishes the second. duly's witnesses are **normalized**: rather than print whichever satisfying assignment the solver happened to return, the verifier pins each symbol to the first value from a fixed ladder drawn from the pack's own vocabulary that keeps the query satisfiable, so the same pack prints the same witness on every run, machine, and solver version. See [spec/pack-verification.md](../spec/pack-verification.md).

**Receipt verification** — Checking a single receipt, by whoever is holding it. Three independent checks, kept separate because they establish different things and fail for different reasons. *Hash* — recompute SHA-256 over the receipt's canonical body and compare it to the `receiptSha256` it carries; needs nothing but the receipt, and proves only that the document has not changed since it was sealed. *Fact integrity* — every fact pinned in `inputFacts` is present and hashes to the content hash in its own id. *Replay* — re-adjudicate through `duly_kernel.api.adjudicate` on those facts, that pack version and the receipt's own asOf pair, and compare byte-for-byte. The distinction the three make visible is the one worth having: a receipt whose verdict was edited *and then re-sealed* passes hash and fact integrity and fails only replay, because it is a perfectly consistent record of a decision the rules never made. Sealed, consistent, and false are three different states. Surfaced in the [receipt viewer](../docs/demo_tour.md#12-the-receipt-viewer); the corpus-wide form is replay verification, below.

**Golden corpus, replay verification, and impact analysis** — The committed baseline and the two tools that consume it. The corpus is 351 cases with their golden receipts — 350 seeded, deterministically regenerable synthetic cases plus one born from a real review-queue correction. *Replay verification* re-adjudicates every case and asserts `receiptSha256` is byte-identical — it catches breakage and proves determinism continuously. *Impact analysis* re-adjudicates under the current working-tree packs — or under a candidate pack the caller supplies that is not on disk at all, which is how the [rule studio](#authoring) reports a flip count before the edit is written anywhere — and reports "this change flips N of M historical decisions" with before/after receipts. It catches drift, which a pack's own `expected.yaml` cannot: declared outcomes catch a pack that breaks, only the corpus catches a pack whose meaning moved. See [golden/README.md](../golden/README.md).

## Authoring

**Rule studio** — The demo's surface for the rules rather than the documents (<http://localhost:8788/rules>). It renders each pack's rules as one **decision-table** grid per concluded attribute — rows are rules, columns are the inputs they bind — and lets an author edit cells, rule forms or the YAML text, then run the validator, the pack's declared cases, an ad-hoc case, [impact analysis](#assurance) and [static verification](#assurance) over the draft. Three boundaries are deliberate. The grid is a *projection of the rule IR*, not a DMN document: the [DMN compiler](../spec/dmn.md) is one-way, so a guard relating several bindings appears as a cross-input condition the grid cannot hold rather than being flattened into a cell that would misstate it. A draft lives in the server process and is never written into `rulepacks/` — the studio produces bytes and a diff, and committing is a human act, exactly as with the golden-case export. And a structured edit re-emits the pack from the IR, which drops YAML comments; the studio shows that loss in the file diff instead of hiding it, and keeps a text-editing path that preserves them. Walkthrough: [demo tour §10](demo_tour.md#10-the-rule-studio).

## Review

**Review queue and the correction arc** — Where abstentions go, and the loop that turns the kernel's "I don't know" into labeled knowledge. Receipt abstention entries enqueue as deduplicated, idempotent items; a human either *resolves* one by supplying a human-asserted grounded fact — ingested through the fact store's public API, binding by outranking or [superseding](#time) the machine fact — or *dismisses* it because the abstention was right. The full correction arc — abstain, human corrects, the decision flips on re-adjudication, the resolution is frozen as a replayable `review-NNNN` golden case — closes the loop, and resolved items also export the `(raw_score, correct)` pairs that [calibration](#perception) fits on. Queue semantics and caveats: [review/README.md](../review/README.md).
